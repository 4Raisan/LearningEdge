# Lecture-to-video workflow

## Prepare source material

Accept lecture audio or video, an existing transcript, and supporting materials such as slides, notes, and readings. Transcribe recordings when needed and retain timestamps. Keep page, slide, or section references for supporting material so individual claims can be traced back to their sources. Flag conflicts or unclear passages for review.

## Generate and verify the notes

Use the lecture as source material. Identify the concepts, theories, mechanisms, and relationships it teaches. Group related ideas across passages, drop filler and repetition, and explain the concepts in fresh language. Keep the definitions, reasoning, qualifications, and useful examples needed to understand the material. Check the outline against the sources for missing concepts, unsupported claims, and copied passages. Hold flagged outlines before rendering.

## Generate and verify the video plan

Turn the verified concepts into a narration script and storyboard. For every required concept, record the scene that explains it through narration or visuals. Check for missing, partial, and inaccurate explanations. Revise the script or storyboard and check coverage again before rendering. If automated revisions leave a gap, report it and hold rendering for review.

## Render and check the explainer

Generate narration for each scene before rendering its motion graphics. Use the measured audio duration to determine the frame count and use sentence cues where a visual reveal needs to match a phrase. Generate captions and assemble the scenes. Split content across scenes when a template cannot display it at a readable size.

Let the material determine the runtime, with no fixed time limit. Confirm that the required scenes are present in the finished video and resolve their references to video timestamps. A learner should be able to follow each required point from its source to its note and then to the video segment that explains it.
